TITLE: Steinhoff Explained: How R100 Billion Vanished in Two Days (and How to Protect Your Savings)

DESCRIPTION:
On 5 December 2017 one Steinhoff share cost about R46. Two days later it cost R10. Put R100,000 into Steinhoff alone and you had about R21,900 left. Spread the same R100,000 over 20 companies and the same crash leaves you with about R96,100. That is the lesson of South Africa's biggest corporate fraud: never let one company hold all your savings. Here is the Steinhoff story explained simply: how it grew, the trick investigators found, the four warning signs, who lost, where things stand today, and what it means for your money.

Key figures
- Share: about R46 before the news, R17.61 on 6 Dec 2017 (−61%), R10.00 on 7 Dec (−43% more): about 80% in two days, roughly €10 billion wiped out.
- PwC (summary published 15 Mar 2019): €6.5 billion of fictitious or irregular transactions, 2009–2017 (more than R100 billion).
- Government Employees Pension Fund: about 9.1% of Steinhoff, down from R24.1 billion to R3.1 billion. Pension payments were not cut.
- Settlement: €1.43 billion (about R25 billion), approved Jan 2022; 43,000+ claims worth about €3.2 billion. The shares ceased to exist on 13 Oct 2023.
- Former CEO: 20-year JSE director ban; record R475 million FSCA penalty (20 Mar 2024). Former CFO: 10 years, 5 suspended (Oct 2024).
- Today: Ibex Investment Holdings sold its whole Pepkor stake in July 2025 for about R27 billion to pay debt. Pepkor is still listed, with 6,500+ stores.
- Regulation 28: at most 15% of a retirement fund in one listed company worth R20 billion+. Steinhoff was under 2% of the S&P South Africa 50 (cost the index about 1.4%). TFSA: R46,000 a year.

About the example: Elsie is an illustration. R100,000 at R45.65 is worth about R21,906 at R10. Over 20 companies (R5,000 each, the other 19 assumed flat): about R96,095 (−3.9%); at zero, R95,000.
