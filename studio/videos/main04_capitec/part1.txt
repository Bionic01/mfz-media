[serious] In 2003, a small bank from Stellenbosch had about 25,000 clients. Today it has 26.6 million. That's more than any other bank in South Africa, and about twice as many as the next biggest one.

That bank is Capitec. It started as a small money lender, up against giants. So how did it win?

In this video, you'll get the story in plain words, what it actually did differently, and the lesson for your own pocket: how to work out what your bank really costs you each month, and how to pay less. Let's start from zero.

Our story starts in the year 2000, with a company called PSG, founded by Jannie Mouton.

PSG owned a microlending business. That means small, short loans to ordinary people. It had bought two lenders, SmartFin and FinAid, with 68 small branches between them.

Then PSG decided to turn that business into a bank. Michiel le Roux became the first chief executive. He brought in experienced bankers, including Riaan Stassen. And a banker called Gerrie Fourie joined them too. Many years later, he would run the whole company.

On the first of March 2001, Capitec was founded, in Stellenbosch.

That was a brave move. Lending people money is one thing. Asking them to trust you with their salary, every single month, is something else.

Now, here's the important part. Who was Capitec for?

The big banks already had the wealthier clients. So Capitec went after a different market: lower-income workers, and people who'd never had a bank account at all.

Capitec describes its idea in one sentence: banking should be simple, transparent and affordable.

Simple means one main account, not a menu of twenty. Transparent means you can see what you pay. And affordable means low fees. Today, Capitec's monthly account fee is R7.50.

It sounds obvious. But it was a big bet. When each client pays only a few rand, a bank only makes real money with a huge number of clients.

And the clients came.

From about 25,000 clients in 2003, Capitec grew to 24.1 million by February 2025, and to 26.6 million by August this year. That's more than a thousand times bigger.

Branches grew from 266 to 885.

And profit followed. Capitec's headline earnings, a main measure of profit, were R30 million in 2003. In the year to February 2026, they were R16.8 billion. And in just the six months to August this year, R9.5 billion, up 19%.

The founders stepped back over the years. Gerrie Fourie, the last founder running the company day to day, retired in July 2025, and Graham Lee took over as chief executive.
