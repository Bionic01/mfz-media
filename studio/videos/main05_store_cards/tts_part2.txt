Back to Thandi.

The TV normally costs 6,250 rand. On Black Friday, it's 5,000 rand. That's 20% off. She "saves" 1,250 rand. She already has a store card, so she puts it on the card, and pays it off over twelve months.

Let's say her card charges the maximum the law allows. Her instalment, with interest, is about 466 rand a month. Add the 69 rand service fee, and the credit life insurance, and she pays about 548 rand a month.

After twelve months, she's paid about 6,570 rand.

That's about 1,570 rand more than the sale price. Her 1,250 rand discount is gone, and she's paid more than the full price on the shelf.

And remember that version that costs almost double? Here it is.

The salesperson says: "Make it thirty-six months, and it's only about 271 rand a month." It sounds cheaper. It isn't.

Over thirty-six months, the interest, the fees and the insurance keep running. Thandi would pay almost 9,750 rand. For a 5,000 rand TV. Nearly two TVs, and she'd still be paying for it when the next two Black Fridays come round.

Here's why. On thirty-six months, the 69 rand fee alone adds up to 2,484 rand. That's half the TV again, before a cent of interest.

Let's put them side by side. Cash: 5,000 rand. Twelve months: about 6,570 rand. Thirty-six months: almost 9,750 rand. Same TV. Same shop. Same Black Friday.

Your card may charge less than the maximum, so check your own statement. But the pattern is always the same: the longer you take, the more you pay.

So here are the five traps to watch for this Black Friday. Number five is the one that keeps people paying for years.

Trap one: only looking at the monthly amount. "Only 271 rand a month" hides the total. Always ask: what will I pay in total?

Trap two: the fake discount. A "was" price isn't always what the item really sold for. Check what it cost a few weeks ago.

Trap three: a higher limit. Around sales, you may be offered more credit. A bigger limit isn't more money. It's more debt you're allowed to take.

Trap four: the insurance you didn't choose. The law says you can use your own policy instead, as long as it gives the same cover. If you already have cover, ask.

Trap five: paying only the minimum. The minimum keeps the account open. It doesn't clear the debt. Interest keeps running on everything you still owe.
