First, what is a store card?

A store card is a loan from the shop, or from a lender working with the shop. You get a limit, say 8,000 rand. You can buy up to that amount now, and pay it back later, a bit every month.

The law calls this a credit facility. It works like a credit card, but you can usually only use it at that shop, or that group of shops.

Why do shops like them so much? Because with a store card, the sale doesn't end at the till. Every month you take to pay, the lender earns interest and fees. That's the business. And it's why the card is offered to you at the till, right when you're excited about the deal.

And here's the part most people miss. The price on the shelf is not the price you pay. When you pay later, you pay the shelf price, plus the cost of borrowing. That cost has three parts.

Part one: interest.

Interest is the price of borrowing money, as a percentage of what you owe, every year. The National Credit Act sets a maximum for store cards: the Reserve Bank's repo rate plus 14%.

Right now the repo rate is 7.25%. So the maximum is 21.25% a year. To feel that: owe 5,000 rand for a whole year without paying anything, and the interest alone is more than 1,060 rand.
