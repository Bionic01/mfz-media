TITLE:
Black Friday Store Cards Explained: The Real Cost of Buying on Credit (South Africa 2026)

DESCRIPTION:
Thandi saved R1,250 on a TV on Black Friday. Twelve months later she'd paid about R6,570 for it, more than the full price before the sale. On 36 months it would have been almost R9,750. Here's where every rand went: how a store card works, the three costs nobody tells you about at the till (interest, the monthly service fee and credit life insurance), the legal maximums in 2026, the five traps to dodge, and how to shop this Black Friday (Friday 27 November 2026) without the trap.

The legal maximums for store cards and credit cards (credit facilities) today:
- Interest: the Reserve Bank's repo rate + 14% a year. With the repo rate at 7.25% (since 25 September 2026), that's 21.25% a year. It moves when the repo rate moves; the next decision is on 19 November 2026.
- Monthly service fee: R60 + VAT = R69 a month
- Credit life insurance: R4.50 a month per R1,000 you owe
- Initiation fee (new accounts only): up to R1,050 + VAT = R1,207.50
You may use your own credit life policy instead of the one offered, as long as it gives the cover the law requires.

About the example: Thandi is an illustration, priced at the legal maximums (21.25% a year, R69 a month service fee, credit life at R4.50 per R1,000 on the balance, equal monthly payments, no initiation fee because she already has the card). R5,000 over 12 months: R466.17 instalment + R69 + credit life (about R12.58 a month on average) = about R548 a month, R6,572.98 in total. Over 24 months: R8,137.14. Over 36 months: R189.02 instalment, about R271 a month all in, R9,747.23 in total, of which R2,484 is service fees. Saving R548 a month for 10 months gives R5,480 (no interest assumed). Your store card may charge less than the maximum: check your statement and your credit agreement.
