Step one: find your rate. Open your bond, car or credit card statement and look for "prime", plus or minus something. If it says "fixed", your rate won't move for now.

Step two: work out your number. Here's a rule of thumb. On a twenty-year bond, every quarter percent is about 17 rand a month for every 100,000 rand you owe. Owe 800,000 rand? That's eight times 17 rand: about 136 rand. On a six-year car loan, it's about 13 rand per 100,000 rand. So a 300,000 rand car loan moves by about 38 rand.

Step three: build a buffer. Before Thursday, put that number aside in your budget, as if the rate had already gone up. For Naledi, that's 204 rand. If it doesn't go up, you've saved it.

Step four: pay the expensive debt first. Credit cards and store cards cost up to 21.25% a year. Every rand you pay off there beats almost any savings account.

Step five: make the rate work for you. When rates rise, check what your savings earn, and compare accounts. Some pay much more than others.

And here's the habit I promised. If Naledi pays the higher amount, 12,386 rand, even while her rate is still 10.75%, her bond is paid off about a year early. And she saves more than 112,000 rand in interest. All from paying 204 rand extra a month.

Let's recap.

The repo rate is what the Reserve Bank charges banks. Banks add 3.5% to get prime. Your loans are priced from prime.

A quarter percent on a 1.2 million rand bond is about 204 rand a month. Your rule of thumb: 17 rand per 100,000 rand.

If you owe more than you save, a hike costs you more than it pays you.

Pay the expensive debt first, and if you can, pay a little extra on your bond.

And whatever happens on Thursday, up, down or no change, you now know your number.
