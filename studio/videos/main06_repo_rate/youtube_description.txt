TITLE: Repo Rate Explained: What a 0.25% Change Does to Your Bond, Car and Savings (South Africa 2026)

DESCRIPTION:
A quarter of a percent on the repo rate adds about R204 a month to a R1.2 million bond, about R53 to a R413,000 car loan, and only about R25 a year to R10,000 in savings. The Reserve Bank's next decision is on Thursday 19 November 2026. Here's what the repo rate is, how it reaches your payslip in three stops, why the Reserve Bank raises it, and how to work out your own number in ten seconds.

Today's rates (SARB, 13 November 2026)
- Repo rate (the SARB now calls it the policy rate): 7.25%, since 25 September 2026. It was 6.75% before May 2026, then 7.00% from 29 May.
- Prime lending rate: 10.75% (repo + 3.5 percentage points, a fixed gap since 2001).
- Legal maximum on credit cards and store cards: repo + 14% = 21.25% a year.
- Inflation target: 3%, with a tolerance band of 1 percentage point either side. Inflation (CPI) was 4.5% in September 2026.

Rule of thumb
- 20-year bond: every 0.25% is about R17 a month for every R100,000 you owe (R16.96 at 10.75% to 11%).
- 6-year car loan: about R13 a month per R100,000 (R12.78).

About the example: Naledi is an illustration. Both loans are at prime for simplicity, with equal monthly payments. Bond R1,200,000 over 20 years: R12,182.75 a month at 10.75%, R12,386.26 at 11.00% (+R203.51 a month, about R2,442 a year); a cut to 10.50% would mean about R202 less. Car R413,000 over 72 months: R7,808.29 at 10.75%, R7,861.07 at 11.00% (+R52.78). Credit card R20,000: +0.25% is about R4.17 a month. Savings R10,000: +0.25% is R25 a year, if your bank passes it on (not every account does). Since May 2026 (prime 10.25% to 10.75%) the bond instalment rose by about R403 a month. Paying R12,386.26 a month on the bond while the rate is 10.75% pays it off in 227 months instead of 240 and saves about R112,600 in interest. The R1.2 million is BetterBond's average first-time buyer home loan (12 months to July 2026); R413,000 is TransUnion's estimated average new vehicle loan (Q4 2025). Your own rate, term and bank may differ: check your statement.
