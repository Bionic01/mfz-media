So Sipho has about fifteen thousand two hundred rand. What should he do with it? Here's an order that works for most people.

One: pay off expensive debt. A store card or a credit card can legally charge up to the repo rate plus fourteen percentage points a year. That's over twenty percent right now. Say Sipho owes six thousand rand on a store card at twenty percent a year. That's about twelve hundred rand a year in interest. Paying off a debt like that is like earning twenty percent on your money, guaranteed. No investment can promise you that.

Two: build your emergency fund. If your car breaks down or you lose your job in January, you don't want to borrow. Aim for a few months of expenses, saved where you can reach them quickly. There's a full guide on that on this channel.

Three: if you have no expensive debt and an emergency fund, look at a tax-free savings account. From March 2026, you can put in up to forty-six thousand rand a year, and five hundred thousand over your lifetime. The growth is tax-free. But go over the limit, and SARS charges forty percent on the extra. And if you take money out, you can't put it back without using up more of your limit.

Four: a retirement annuity. Money you put into a retirement fund can be deducted from your taxable income, up to twenty-seven and a half percent of your income, and up to four hundred and thirty thousand rand a year. Because Sipho's bonus pushed him into the twenty-six percent step, every rand he puts into a retirement annuity this tax year saves him twenty-six cents in tax. Put in five thousand rand, and he gets about one thousand three hundred rand back from SARS when he files his tax return. Just remember, a retirement annuity is for retirement: most of the money stays locked in until you retire.

And five: spend some of it, on purpose. It's December. Plan what you'll spend on family and gifts, write the number down, and stick to it. Because January is long, and the next payday can feel very far away.

And three bonus mistakes to avoid.

One: spending it before it arrives. Buying on credit in November because the bonus is coming means part of it is gone before you see it, plus interest.

Two: planning with the full amount. Plan with what lands in your account after tax, not the number in your contract.

And three: forgetting about January. December pay often comes early, and the next payday can be weeks away. Keep something back.
