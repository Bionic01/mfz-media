Now the debt piled up. Eskom was paying billions in interest every year, while its old power stations kept breaking down.

And 2023 was the worst year of all. South Africa had close to seven thousand hours of load shedding. Shops ran generators. Factories cut shifts. Families cooked by candlelight.

That same year, Parliament passed the Eskom Debt Relief Act. The government would give Eskom up to two hundred and fifty-four billion rand over three years, to help pay its debt. That's taxpayers' money. Your money.

And there was another hole. Many municipalities buy electricity from Eskom, sell it to you, and then don't pay Eskom in full. By March 2026, municipalities owed Eskom one hundred and eleven point six billion rand. That's up almost eighteen percent in one year.

So when you pay your municipality for electricity, part of that money is supposed to reach Eskom. In too many towns, it doesn't.

So where does Eskom stand today? The news is better than it's been in years.

Load shedding has mostly stopped. In its last financial year, to March 2026, Eskom had just four days of load shedding, twenty-six hours in total. Its power stations are working more reliably: they were available about sixty-five percent of the time, up from about sixty-one, though still short of Eskom's own target of seventy.

And Eskom made a profit: thirty point three billion rand, more than double the year before. That's its second profit in a row, after eight years of losses. Its net debt fell to three hundred and thirteen billion rand.

But there's a new problem. Eskom is selling less electricity: six percent less in a year. Mines and smelters are using less, and many homes and businesses have put up solar panels.

And when a company sells less, but still has to pay for its power stations and its debt, it has to charge more for every unit it sells.

That's why the electricity minister said in August 2026 that prices had risen far faster than inflation, and that the era of double-digit increases should end. And he had a message for municipalities that don't pay: you are not allowed to punish those who are paying, for those who are not paying.
