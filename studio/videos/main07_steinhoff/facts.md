## 2. What PwC found (published by Steinhoff, 15 Mar 2019)

Primary source: "Overview of Forensic Investigation", Steinhoff International Holdings N.V., 15 March 2019 (PDF copy hosted by Corruption Watch): https://www.corruptionwatch.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/overview-of-forensic-investigation.pdf

- **Total fictitious and/or irregular transactions: €6,506,596,428 (about €6.5bn)** over **FY2009–FY2017**.
- In rand: Business Day's headline was "inflated profits by **more than R100bn**" (15 Mar 2019): https://www.businesslive.co.za/bd/companies/retail-and-consumer/2019-03-15-steinhoff-executives-inflated-profits-by-more-than-r100bn-pwc-finds/ (The "R106bn" figure is UNVERIFIED. Say "more than R100 billion".)
- **How it worked, simply:** income was created inside intermediary holding companies and booked as deals with parties that were "said to be, and made to appear to be, third party entities independent of the Steinhoff Group". In fact these parties appeared "closely related to and/or have strong indications of control by the same small group of people". The money was then pushed down to struggling operating companies as "contributions". The four categories were: profit and asset creation (brands, intellectual property, know-how), asset overstatement, inflated rents and royalties, and contributions. Many receivables were never paid, and documents were "in many instances ... created after the fact and backdated".
- **Main counterparty groups (EUR):** Talgarth Group (excl. Triton) 4.16bn; TG Group 1.02bn; GT Global Trademarks 0.66bn; Triton 0.42bn; Tulett 0.17bn; plus smaller amounts.
- **Who:** "a small group of Steinhoff Group former executives and other non-Steinhoff executives, led by a senior management executive". The summary does **not name** that person.
- Steinhoff itself later took a write-down of about **$12bn** (2018; Wikipedia, secondary).

## 4. People and cases

**Markus Jooste (CEO; born 22 Jan 1961, died 21 Mar 2024, aged 63)**
- **JSE:** R15m in fines (2 × R7.5m), a public censure and a **20-year ban** from being a director of a listed company. The Financial Services Tribunal confirmed these in Oct 2023. Tribunal line: "It was not because of Covid or some eruption on Mount Krakatoa." (Business Day, 10–11 Oct 2023): https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/companies/retail-and-consumer/2023-10-10-steinhoffs-collapse-not-due-to-mount-krakatoa-says-tribunal-as-it-confirms-joostes-20-year-ban/
- **FSCA insider-trading fine:** originally R161.6m, later revised to **R20m** (Daily Maverick, 9 May 2023; The Citizen, 20 Mar 2024).
- **FSCA R475m administrative penalty, announced 20 Mar 2024.** It was the largest the FSCA had ever imposed on an individual, and it includes R10m towards investigation costs. It was for false, misleading or deceptive statements in Steinhoff's annual financial statements for 2014–2016 and the H1 2017 report (Financial Markets Act s81). The FSCA said it would add a criminal referral to the NPA's investigation.
  - The Citizen, 20 Mar 2024: https://www.citizen.co.za/business/fsca-fines-markus-jooste-r475-million-refers-case-to-hawks
  - Moonstone, 20 Mar 2024: https://www.moonstone.co.za/markus-jooste-fined-r475m-for-publishing-misleading-steinhoff-financials/
- **Arrest warrants and death:** the NPA and Hawks secured warrants. Jooste was notified on the afternoon of **21 Mar 2024** and told to hand himself over (reportedly by Fri 22 Mar). He died on **21 Mar 2024** near Hermanus. Police registered an inquest docket. *Editorial note: do not describe the manner of death. Say "he died the day after the FSCA fine, before he could appear in court".* The planned charges reportedly related to share-price manipulation, misrepresentations to business people (including Christo Wiese) and years of misstated financial statements (Daily Maverick, 21 Mar 2024). His SA criminal case fell away on his death.
  - Polity / Bloomberg, 22 Mar 2024: https://www.polity.org.za/article/ex-steinhoff-ceo-jooste-death-came-after-arrest-warrant-issued-2024-03-22
  - Daily Maverick, 21 Mar 2024: https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2024-03-21-le-roux-mbikiwa-trengove-npas-a-team-was-ready-to-charge-markus-jooste/
  - EWN, 21 Mar 2024 (inquest docket): https://www.ewn.co.za/2024/03/21/police-confirm-inquest-docket-registered-into-ex-steinhoff-ceo-markus-joostes-death
- **Germany:** Oldenburg prosecutors had indicted Jooste and others in late 2020 (revealed in Mar 2021, Bloomberg via BizNews). He never appeared for trial in Germany.

## 7. Warning signs before the crash

1. **Nov 2015 raid and German probe:** on 26 Nov 2015, Oldenburg authorities raided Steinhoff's European HQ in Westerstede, Germany, and an investigation into suspected accounting fraud began. Steinhoff announced the raid on **4 Dec 2015**, days before its Frankfurt listing (Wikipedia, Markus Jooste article, citing Fin24, 4 Dec 2015; secondary). By Dec 2017 the probe covered four current and former managers suspected of overstating revenues.
2. **Aug 2017, Manager Magazin:** the German magazine reported that Jooste himself was under investigation for alleged accounting fraud. Shares fell **5.7% to a record low of €3.99 in Frankfurt** and about **9% on the JSE** by midday. Steinhoff rejected the claims. BusinessTech, 24 Aug 2017: https://businesstech.co.za/news/business/194412/steinhoff-shares-fall-sharply-amid-reports-of-fraud-investigation/
3. **2007, analysts:** JPMorgan analysts questioned why Steinhoff's accounts lacked "pivotal information" about where revenue came from, and Steinhoff accused them of "asking for inside information" (Bloomberg via Accounting Today, 18 Dec 2017).
4. **Related-party and complex deals:** examples include a long-running dispute with Austrian partner Andreas Seifert over ownership of the POCO chain, and deals with entities linked to insiders. One was the Genesis/Kika deal tied to ex-CFO Siegmar Schmidt: Steinhoff lent €375m and later bought property from Genesis at a €77m premium (as summarised from Viceroy by Business Day). **Timing note:** Viceroy Research's Steinhoff report came out in the days **after** the 5 Dec 2017 announcement, so it was **not** a pre-crash warning. The exact publication date is UNVERIFIED. Business Day, 15 Dec 2017: https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/companies/financial-services/2017-12-15-how-did-short-seller-viceroy-dig-out-the-info-to-make-a-killing-on-steinhoff/
5. **Too-fast growth by acquisition:** Pepkor ($5.7bn, 2015), Poundland (£610m, 2016), Mattress Firm ($3.8bn, 2016). By Aug 2016 Steinhoff had **6,500 stores, 40 brands, 30 countries and about 90,000 staff** (Wikipedia; secondary).
6. **Results delayed and auditor would not sign:** the 2017 results due on 6 Dec 2017 were postponed after Deloitte flagged irregularities. Steinhoff later said its 2015 and 2016 financial statements "can no longer be relied upon" (BusinessTech, 2 Jan 2018: https://businesstech.co.za/news/?p=217425).

## 8. Diversification facts for South Africans

  - **15%** if the company's market cap is R20bn or more
  - **10%** between R2bn and R20bn
  - **5%** below R2bn
  - Overall equity limit: **75%**. Overall per-entity limit across all instruments: 25%.

## Benchmark (17 Nov 2026, before writing)

Chrome (storyboard frames) was not available in this cloud run, so the benchmark used the studio's earlier frame-by-frame notes (engine/README.md "Look v4": Kurzgesagt "We Finally Know More About Humanity's First Civilization", "AI Just Crossed the Terrifying Line", "How Are Memories Stored Inside Your Brain?"; PolyMatter "Zimbabwe's Currency Crisis"; Fireship "The worst week in cybersecurity history"), Kurzgesagt's own description of its process (kurzgesagt.org/youtube, read 17 Nov 2026) and the treatment of this exact story in Showmax's "Steinheist" (2022, reviewed by Daily Maverick 23 Sep 2022 and BizNews), which is a talking-head documentary, not a faceless explainer.

What we adopt (techniques only, nothing copied):
- **Hook (PolyMatter / Kurzgesagt):** open on one person in a concrete moment, told in the present tense ("Tuesday night, the fifth of December, 2017. Meet Elsie."), then the number, then the question; the answer to the title is said at 1:05.
- **Pacing (Fireship):** a new picture every 2–5 s (150 shots in 9:12, none over 6.3 s), stamp words for the punchline numbers (−78%, NOT SIGNED, POSTPONED, 20-YEAR BAN), receipts on screen (the PwC overview line and the 13 Oct 2023 notice, key words highlighted).
- **How the key idea is shown (Kurzgesagt):** one metaphor carried through: the spaza shop that "sells" stock to a cousin's shop it secretly controls, which then match-cuts into the Steinhoff tower; the lesson is shown with the same towers (one tower vs twenty towers vs the 40-company index).
- **Depth, colour and motion in 2D (Kurzgesagt):** flat rounded shapes with gradient rim light and long soft shadows, 5 parallax layers (sky, far city or hills, mid layer, subject, out-of-focus foreground motes), the camera always pushing or drifting, twinkling stars and flickering windows, a palette per chapter (night for the cold open, sunset for the rise, purple for the trick, teal for the warning signs, ember for the crash, mint for "what this means for you").
- Steinheist's lesson for us: the story drifts when it follows the people; we keep it on Elsie and her money and say the lesson by 1:05.
