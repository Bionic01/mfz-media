# The two-pot retirement system: what you really get when you withdraw (South Africa, 2026)

Voice: Darius (ElevenLabs eleven_v4), one take. Target ~8.5–9 minutes.
Every [KEY] marks a scene cue for the animation. Facts: facts.md (verified 5 Nov 2026).

[cold_open]
[serious] From September 2024 to February 2026, SARS approved about R79 billion in withdrawals from South Africans' retirement savings. And the tax on that money came to about R21 billion.

That's the two-pot retirement system. It lets you take some of your retirement money early, without resigning. But it's easy to get wrong, and most people don't know what they'll really get in their hand.

In this video, you'll learn how the three pots work, what a withdrawal really costs you, the traps to avoid, and how to withdraw the right way, if you need to. Let's start from zero.

[ch1_why]
First, why did the rules change?

Before September 2024, most people had only one way to get their retirement money early. They had to resign. Leave your job, and you could cash out your whole fund. Many people did exactly that. They changed jobs, took the cash, and reached retirement with almost nothing saved.

So the government changed the rules. The two-pot system started on 1 September 2024. The idea is simple: give you a little access for emergencies, and lock the rest away, so it's still there when you retire.

[ch2_pots]
Now, the pots. It's called two-pot… but there are actually three.

The vested pot. This is everything you saved up to 31 August 2024. It doesn't get new contributions, but it keeps growing, and the old rules still apply to it.

The savings pot. From 1 September 2024, one third of your contributions go here. This is the pot you can take money from before you retire.

The retirement pot. The other two thirds go here. This money is locked until you retire.

Think of it like this. The savings pot is a small emergency door. The retirement pot is a locked safe.

To get things started, funds moved some seed money into everyone's savings pot. It was a once-off 10% of your vested pot, up to a maximum of R30,000. It doesn't happen every year.

[ch3_rules]
So, what are the rules for the savings pot?

You can only withdraw once per tax year. The tax year runs from 1 March to the end of February. And the smallest amount you can take is R2,000.

But here's what many people miss. A withdrawal is not tax-free. It's added to your income for the year, and taxed at your normal tax rate. Your fund also charges an admin fee, often around R300. And if you owe SARS money, SARS takes that first, before you see a cent.

And remember, you don't have to withdraw at all. If you leave your savings pot alone, it simply keeps growing with the rest of your fund.

[ch4_example]
Let's make that real. Say you earn R25,000 a month, so R300,000 a year. You're under 65, and you withdraw R20,000 from your savings pot.

At that income, your tax rate is 26%. So SARS takes about R5,200 of your R20,000. Take off a fee of about R345, and you're left with around R14,450 in your hand.

So you didn't really get R20,000. You got about R14,450.

And there's a hidden cost. If that R20,000 had stayed invested, growing at 5% a year above inflation, in 30 years it could be worth about R86,000 in today's money. [softly] That's what the withdrawal really costs your future self. It's only an illustration, not a promise.

[ch5_retire]
Now, the retirement pot. This one is strict.

You can't take it out before you retire. Not if you resign. Not if you're retrenched. It stays in the fund, or it moves to another fund. When you retire, it has to buy you a monthly income, called an annuity.

There are a few exceptions. If you pass away, it goes to your beneficiaries. If you emigrate, you can get it after three years in a row as a non-resident for tax. And if your savings are small when you retire, you may be able to take it all in cash. Your fund can tell you if you qualify.

[ch6_vested]
What about the vested pot, the money from before September 2024?

The old rules still apply. If you're in a pension or provident fund and you resign, you can usually still cash out your vested pot. It's taxed, but the first R27,500 you withdraw in your lifetime is tax-free. And when you retire, up to R550,000 of your cash lump sum can be tax-free.

If you have a retirement annuity, it's stricter. In most cases, you can't touch it before age 55.

[ch7_traps]
[serious] Now, the four traps.

Trap one: the tax surprise. Your fund takes tax off when it pays you. But your final tax is worked out when you file your tax return. If you have other income, you could still owe SARS more later.

Trap two: the flat fee. A fee of R320 is small on R20,000. But on a R2,000 withdrawal, that same fee eats 16% of your money.

Trap three: the habit. Once it's easy, people keep coming back. At Momentum, 62% of the claims since 1 March 2026 were people's third withdrawal. Every time, your retirement gets smaller.

Trap four: scams. Only apply through your fund's official website or app, or through HR at work. Never share your OTP or your password with anyone who offers to help you claim.

[ch8_when]
So, should you withdraw?

The savings pot is meant for real emergencies. Not holidays. Not shopping. Not things that can wait.

But life happens. If the choice is between a withdrawal and a loan shark charging huge interest, a withdrawal can be the lesser evil. Just know the real cost before you do it.

Before you withdraw, ask yourself three questions. Is this a real emergency? Is there a cheaper option, like a payment plan? And do I know exactly what I'll get after tax and fees?

The best protection is your own emergency fund, in a normal savings account. Even a small one. That way, your retirement money can keep growing.

[ch9_how]
If you do need to withdraw, here's how.

Step one. Check that you have at least R2,000 in your savings pot, and that you haven't withdrawn yet this tax year.

Step two. Make sure you're registered for tax, your tax returns are all filed, and your name, ID number and bank details match everywhere. If they don't, SARS can reject it.

Step three. Apply through your fund's website or app, or through HR at work.

Step four. Your fund asks SARS for a tax directive. That tells the fund how much tax to take off, and any money you owe SARS.

Step five. The fund pays the rest into your bank account. Many funds pay within about two weeks, but some take longer.

And one tip: don't apply at the very end of February. A late claim might count against the next tax year.

[ch10_news]
What's changing? Government is talking about letting retrenched workers get some money from the retirement pot, if they're in serious hardship. But right now, it's only a proposal. It is not the law. So be careful of anyone who tells you otherwise.

[ch11_recap]
Let's recap.

There are three pots: vested, savings and retirement. You can withdraw from the savings pot once a tax year, from R2,000. Every withdrawal is taxed, has a fee, and costs you future growth. The retirement pot is locked until you retire. And always claim through official channels.
