TITLE:
How to Build an Emergency Fund in South Africa (2026): How Much You Need and Where to Keep It

DESCRIPTION:
What happens if your car breaks down tomorrow and the bill is R10,000? In this beginner guide you'll learn what an emergency fund is, exactly how big yours should be, where to keep it so it's safe and earns interest, and how to start from zero with just R200 a month.

The basics:
- An emergency fund is money for surprises only: losing your job, a medical bill, a car repair, a burst geyser, a funeral
- A common rule of thumb: 3 to 6 months of your ESSENTIAL expenses (not your salary)
- Build it in 3 levels: a R2,000 starter fund, then 1 month, then 3 to 6 months
- Keep it in a separate bank savings account (split into instant access and a notice account as it grows)
- Bank deposits are covered by the Corporation for Deposit Insurance (CODI) up to R100,000 per person per bank. Money market unit trusts are investments and are not covered
- If you're under 65, the first R23,800 of interest you earn each year is tax-free (2026/27)
- Don't keep it in cash at home, in shares, or in your TFSA

About the example: Thandi's essentials are R10,000 a month, so 3 months is R30,000 and 6 months is R60,000. The growth example assumes an account paying 6% a year, compounded monthly. That's an illustration only, not a quote: check your own bank's rate. R200 a month for 10 months grows to about R2,045; then R1,000 a month passes R30,000 about 26 months later. The two-pot example: someone earning R25,000 a month (26% tax bracket in 2026/27) who withdraws R10,000 pays about R2,600 tax, plus the fund's admin fee.
