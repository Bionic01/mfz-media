And if you're joining someone else's stokvel, ask five questions before you pay anything.

Can I see the constitution? Where is the money kept, and who has to sign before money comes out? Can I see last year's records? What happens if I need to leave early? And when, exactly, do I get paid?

If the answer to any of these is, don't worry, trust us, be careful. And never pay your contribution into someone's personal account.

One more warning. A savings stokvel doesn't make your money grow by itself. If someone promises your stokvel will double your money, or pays old members with new members' money, that's not a stokvel. That's a scheme. Walk away.

Let's recap.

A stokvel is a group of people who save together, by their own rules. More than eleven million South Africans belong to one, saving about fifty billion rand a year.

It works because saving becomes a promise to people you know. But that trust is also the risk: no written rules, money in one person's account, and cash at home.

So protect it. A signed constitution. A separate stokvel bank account. Two of three signatures for every payment. Records everyone can see. And membership of na-SAH-sa.
