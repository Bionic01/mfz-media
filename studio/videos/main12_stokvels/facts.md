## Size
- Ipsos, "Stokvels remain the untapped 'human banks' of South Africa", 13 Mar 2024: a R50 billion sector; more than 800,000 stokvel groups; 11 million members (citing NASASA); "cash remains the preferred mode of operation for most stokvels"; "persistent mistrust of traditional banking institutions, fuelled by perceptions of high fees", lengthy account opening, inflexible withdrawal restrictions, hidden charges; younger generations and higher income brackets joining; "specialised stokvels are surging in popularity" (weddings, investments, property, holidays).

## The word
- Dictionary of South African English, "stokvel": an adaptation of South African English "stockfair"; the Africanized pronunciation of "stock-fair" led to the spelling. https://dsae.co.za/entry/stokvel/e06916

## Risks and protection (EWN, 1 Jul 2026, Warren Ingram, Galileo Capital)
https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/07/01/how-a-well-run-stokvel-can-help-you-save-and-invest-your-money
- Almost every stokvel problem: "Nothing was written down."
- Money in one person's account is exposed to that person's theft, death, divorce or creditors.
- Constitution signed by every member: contributions and due dates, consequences for falling behind or leaving, chairperson and treasurer roles, how and when payouts happen. Templates from NASASA or the Stokvel Academy.
- Dedicated stokvel account (most major banks offer one). "Insist on at least two of three signatories to approve any payment."
- Records everyone can see; read the balance at every meeting; one or two other members check statements against records every few months.
- Money paid out every December: safety and access first, e.g. a fixed deposit or money market account. Horizons over 3 years: "you can consider low-cost unit trusts or exchange-traded funds."

## The law
- Banks Act, Notice 1176 of 2006 (designation of activities not falling within "the business of a bank", common bond groups), para 3: conditions include that the group be "a member of or be affiliated to the National Stokvels Association of South Africa" (or a similar body approved by the Registrar of Banks), benefits not provided only by way of loans, accounting records in an official language, a fixed financial year-end, annual financial statements within 120 days, and limits by total subscriptions (R3 million; R3-30 million with an accountant/auditor).
  https://www.acts.co.za/banks_/n1176_3__conditions
- Voice: "a stokvel group that holds its members' savings stays outside the banking rules only if it meets certain conditions, including belonging to NASASA or a similar approved body, and keeping proper accounting records."

## Our own reasoning (no figures)
- Rotating stokvel risk: the first member paid still has to keep paying; if they stop, later members carry the loss. So write the payout order and the default rule down.
- "Double your money" promises, or paying old members with new members' money: a scheme, not a stokvel.
