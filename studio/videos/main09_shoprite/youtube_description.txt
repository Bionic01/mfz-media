Shoprite sold almost R271 billion of goods in its last financial year: about R5 billion every week. Yet of every R100 you spend there, it keeps only about R6 as trading profit. So how did a business that started with 8 stores and R1 million in 1979 become worth close to R200 billion, with a Shoprite, Checkers or Usave in almost every town? Follow Lerato (an illustration) through a month-end Saturday: the money counter by the door, the trolley, and the till. Then the lesson: what a flat fee really costs you, and how to pay less.

Key figures (Shoprite FY2026, 52 weeks to 28 June 2026)
- Sales R270.8 billion (+7.2%); trading profit R16.2 billion; trading margin 5.97% (about R6 in every R100).
- 3,710 stores; about 174,000 employees; cash generated from operations R27.6 billion.
- Checkers Sixty60 sales R25.5 billion (+34.5%); 36.5 million Xtra Savings customers; R18.3 billion in savings given back at the till (Shoprite).
- History: first store in Wynberg, Cape Town (1966); bought in 1979 (8 stores, R1 million); JSE listing 1986 (about R29 million); Checkers 1991 (R55 million); first Soweto store 1994; OK Bazaars 1997 (R1); first Usave 2003; 500th Usave August 2025.
- Money services: Money Market counter-to-counter transfer R9.99 flat per send (up to R5,000 a day), collected at any Shoprite till (checked 19 Nov 2026); Money Market Account to Money Market Account transfers are free (Shoprite, Nov 2025); 4 million+ Money Market Account customers.

About the example: Lerato is an illustration. R9.99 on R200 is about 5%; on R2,000 it's 0.5%. Four R200 sends a month cost R39.96 in fees; one R800 send costs R9.99: about R30 a month, R360 a year, saved. Check the fee on the day before you send.
