Now the second road. Debt review, through a debt counsellor.

This is for people who already can't keep up. The law has a word for it: over-indebted. It means you can't pay all your debts on time.

Here's how it works. You go to a debt counsellor registered with the National Credit Regulator. You fill in a form, and pay a small application fee. The counsellor looks at your income, your living costs and every debt you have. Then they work out a new plan: lower payments, often with lower interest and more time, that leaves you enough to live on.

Your creditors are told. And once they've been told, they can't take you to court over those debts, as long as you stick to the new plan. A magistrate's court then makes the plan an order.

From then on, you make one payment a month. A payment agency splits it between your creditors. Thabo's four debit orders become one. And because the plan is built around what he can actually afford, there's money left for food, transport and rent.

But debt review has a price, and it's more than money. While you're in it, you can't take on new credit. No new loans, no new cards. Your credit record shows that you're under debt review. And you stay in it until the debts in the plan are paid.

There are fees too, and they're capped. The restructuring fee is your first month's payment, up to eight thousand rand before VAT. Then an after-care fee of up to four hundred and fifty rand a month, before VAT. Legal fees come on top.

When you've paid off the debts in the plan, your debt counsellor gives you a clearance certificate. A home loan can still be running. The credit bureaus must then remove the debt review from your record.
